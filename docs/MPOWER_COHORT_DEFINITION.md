# Cohort definitions and release gates

Every source row is retained in the canonical manifest. Empty strings are missing. Participant-level diagnosis requires explicitly configured source values. PD and control responses at any time for the same participant produce `conflicting`; a participant with no explicit binary response remains `unknown`. Unknown/conflicting participants remain in descriptive audits but cannot enter the supervised cohorts. No medication-based inference is permitted.

The authorized syn5511429 baseline survey joins on observed `healthCode`, with strict uniqueness required before linkage. Missing or repeated demographic participant keys fail closed after writing an audit file; they are never silently deduplicated. Participant cardinality is one-to-one and recording cardinality many-to-one. Unmatched voice participants remain in the census. The raw export is never edited. Direct source diagnosis is retained; controls reporting a plausible diagnosis/medication year or explicit pre/post-PD-medication voice timing become `conflicting` under a conservative primary policy. PD medication nonuse alone does not invalidate a PD label. Repeat sensitivity analyses with source labels before finalizing exclusion decisions.

* A: identified, source-record-ID-unique, explicitly binary-labeled source rows with successful decoding. All quality flags retained.
* B: A restricted to sustained phonation, non-near-silent audio, at least 2 s total and at least 2 s Praat pitch-detected material. These thresholds are exploratory and require diagnosis-specific selection-bias review before final adoption. Short voiced intervals do not automatically imply corruption.
* C: B with participant age 50–80, valid ages and known reported gender, using equal PD/control participant counts within 5-year age bins and reported gender. Fixed-seed subsampling preserves all recordings from selected participants. This coarsened matching does not guarantee identical exact ages; inspect residual age differences and sample-size losses. No participant is selected using an acoustic outcome.

Low amplitude, low estimated SNR, clipping and irregular pitch are review flags. They are not primary automatic exclusions. No universal threshold is claimed for dysphonic voices. Sensitivity tables span duration thresholds 2/3/5/7/9 s and pitch-voicing thresholds 1/2/3 s, including unknown-label recordings for technical assessment.

Sequential exclusion flows count remaining recordings and unique participants by diagnosis after each criterion; separate per-recording exclusions permit overlapping reasons. Unprocessed audio is distinguished from decode failure. Pilot counts never represent whole-dataset QC prevalence.

Provisional 70/15/15 splits use fixed seed 20261112. Participant groups connected by exact file/native-PCM/normalized-PCM hashes or an explicitly supplied near-duplicate group are indivisible. Allocation minimizes participant diagnosis-count imbalance; it is approximate with large components and small strata. All sensitivity cohorts reuse A's assignments. Empty partitions and deviations require review. Age/sex stratification is not implemented. All identified participant/recording/hash/near-duplicate groups are verified disjoint across partitions.

The demographics linkage supports provisional supervised pilot cohorts. Full supervised release remains blocked by incomplete full-audio / near-duplicate review. Empty subsets must be reported explicitly, not interpreted as evidence of no usable audio. No transformations have been fitted. Future learned preprocessing must fit training data only.
